\begin{abstract}
Proposer-Builder Separation (PBS) has become a foundational mechanism in Ethereum’s post-Merge architecture, aiming to mitigate Maximal Extractable Value (MEV) centralization and democratize block production. This paper surveys the evolution of PBS from its off-chain implementation through \emph{MEV-Boost} to the emerging in-protocol design known as \emph{Enshrined PBS} (ePBS). We provide an overview of the motivation behind PBS, its operational flow, and the associated trust assumptions between proposers, builders, and relays. The survey highlights key challenges such as censorship resistance, relay centralization, and proposer-builder collusion, then examines how recent research and proposals—including EIP-7732—seek to enshrine PBS within Ethereum’s consensus. Finally, we discuss the broader implications of these designs for decentralization, validator incentives, and protocol sustainability, identifying open questions and future research directions in enshrined block markets.
\end{abstract}
