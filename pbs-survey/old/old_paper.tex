% \section{Introduction}
% In proof-of-stake Ethereum, validators (proposers) receive on-chain rewards for proposing blocks, but can earn significantly more by exploiting Maximal Extractable Value (MEV) from transaction ordering.  To distribute MEV opportunities, Ethereum adopted Proposer-Builder Separation (PBS) via the MEV-Boost relay system.  In MEV-Boost, validators outsource block construction to off-chain \emph{builders}  through relays who assemble high-value blocks, submit a sealed bid, and then deliver the full block after the proposer commits.  This separation flattens the playing field among validators: proposers no longer need to run sophisticated builder algorithms, and instead accept the highest bid without seeing the transactions.  However, the current PBS ecosystem introduces new trust and centralization issues.

% \subsection{Proposer-Builder Separation (PBS)}
% PBS separates the roles of \emph{building} a block (assembling transactions for maximal revenue) from \emph{proposing} it to the consensus layer.  Intuitively, block-building benefits from private information and computation. Under PBS, builders produce candidate blocks and offer to pay the proposer for their block’s inclusion. The proposer accepts the highest bid among builders, then signs its header and publishes it. Conceptually, PBS aims to delegate MEV advantages to the \textit{builder} side, so validators (proposers) simply ensure validity without needing the same MEV strategies. In the context of two entities - builder and proposer there are a few attacks that each of the party can leverage to exploit in the simple naive communication between the two parties.
